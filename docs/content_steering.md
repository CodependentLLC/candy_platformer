# Content Steering

## Voice

The voice of **Candy Quest Kids** is:

- Warm
- Encouraging
- Playful
- Clear
- Family-friendly
- Storybook-like
- Slightly magical
- Lightly candy-themed

The game should sound like a bedtime adventure through a friendly candy world, not like a sarcastic arcade announcer.

## Story premise

The core premise:

A child wakes up, finds an old door inside the closet, and gets pulled into a candy world they must cross before morning to return home.

New story content should support that premise unless the task explicitly changes the story.

## Tone rules

Use:

- Short sentences
- Clear goals
- Soft excitement
- Gentle encouragement
- Candy and dessert imagery
- Wonder and curiosity

Avoid:

- Mean failure messages
- Scary horror language
- Combat-heavy language
- Adult jokes
- Sarcasm
- Shame
- Gambling-like urgency
- Dark or violent imagery

## Player-facing copy

Good examples:

- `Choose a hero to begin.`
- `Open the door.`
- `The candy path sparkles ahead.`
- `So close! Try the jump again.`
- `Sugar Rush is ready!`
- `You found a hidden special!`
- `The closet door glows in the distance.`
- `Morning is almost here. Keep going!`

Avoid:

- `You failed.`
- `Bad move.`
- `You died.`
- `Grind for rewards.`
- `Buy now.`
- `Punish the enemies.`
- `Only experts can survive this.`

## Naming style

Use candy, dessert, bakery, dream, and storybook names.

Existing or compatible names:

- Lollipop Meadow
- Licorice Lane
- Frosting Falls
- Waffle Woods
- Castle Courtyard Run
- Closet Door Keep
- Gummy Grotto
- Marshmallow Sky
- Sprinkle Bridge
- Caramel Cove
- Jellybean Junction
- Cookie Cavern
- Taffy Tower
- Cupcake Crossing

## Mechanics naming

Keep names readable and kid-friendly.

Good names:

- Sugar Rush
- Sugar Gate
- Bounce Pad
- Cookie Block
- Cupcake Checkpoint
- Candy Trail
- Rainbow Special
- Swift Medal
- Steady Medal
- Specialist Medal

Avoid names that are too abstract or intimidating.

## Tutorial and tip text

Tips should tell players what to do without overexplaining.

Pattern:

1. Name the situation.
2. Give the useful action.
3. Keep it under two short sentences if possible.

Good:

`Sugar gates open and close. Wait for the sweet window, then run through.`

Good:

`Bounce pads launch you higher. Use the candy trail to aim your jump.`

Avoid:

`The gate entity alternates between collision-enabled and collision-disabled states according to a phase timer.`

## Chapter story text

Chapter story should be short and atmospheric.

Good:

`The child steps onto soft frosting stones as the candy castle glows far ahead.`

Avoid:

`In this level, the player must complete a sequence of moving platform challenges and defeat three enemies before reaching the end trigger.`

## Success text

Success text should celebrate what the player learned or accomplished.

Good:

`You crossed the frosting falls and followed the sparkle trail toward the castle.`

Avoid:

`Level complete.`

## Failure and game-over copy

Keep failure light and recoverable.

Good:

- `So close!`
- `Take a breath and try again.`
- `The candy path is still waiting.`
- `That was a tricky jump.`
- `Choose a hero and open the door again.`

Avoid:

- `Game over, you lost.`
- `You are out of chances.`
- `Bad jump.`
- `Failure.`

## UI labels

Use clear labels for essential actions.

Preferred:

- Start Adventure
- Resume
- World Map
- Side Stages
- Choose Hero
- Restart Level
- Sound On
- Sound Off
- Fullscreen
- Pause
- Play Again

Clever labels are okay only when the meaning remains obvious.

## Inclusive character guidance

The Boy and Girl Explorer options should be mechanically equivalent unless the task explicitly asks for cosmetic differences. Do not imply one hero is stronger, braver, faster, or better.

## Content safety

Keep content suitable for kids and families.

Avoid:

- Real-money gambling metaphors
- Manipulative monetization language
- Body-shaming food language
- Frightening closet/bedroom horror escalation
- Graphic injury
- Weapon-forward mechanics
- Harsh punishment framing
